# Quant-Analytics
A probabilistic market research and trading systems framework focused on regime detection, stochastic dynamics, macro constraints, and strategy viability across multi-space financial environments.

<br>

## Quant Skill Roadmap (From Foundations to Research-Level Thinking)

The goal of this roadmap is to convert systems-oriented and probabilistic thinking into mathematical rigor, statistical reasoning, computational implementation, and empirical research discipline.

---

## Phase 1: Mathematical Core

This is the foundation upon which all quantitative research rests.

### 1. Calculus
**Focus areas:**
- Derivatives and partial derivatives
- Multivariable calculus
- Optimization (Gradients and constrained optimization)
- Continuous change

**Key concept:**
$$\frac{d^2x}{dt^2}$$
Understanding acceleration and regime pressure within a system.

### 2. Linear Algebra
Essential for factor models, covariance matrices, PCA, machine learning, and state-space systems.
**Master:**
- Vectors and matrices
- Eigenvalues and eigenvectors
- Matrix decomposition

**Key structure:**
$$Ax = \lambda x$$

### 3. Probability Theory
The fundamental language of quantitative finance.
**Master:**
- Conditional probability
- Bayes Theorem
- Distributions (Expectation and Variance)
- Stochastic processes

**Core Idea (Bayesian Inference):**
$$P(A|B) = \frac{P(B|A)P(A)}{P(B)}$$
*Posterior = Useful Evidence / Total Evidence*

### 4. Statistics
**Focus areas:**
- Estimation and hypothesis testing
- Regression and likelihood functions
- Inference
- Distinguishing signal from noise

## Phase 3: Time Series and Stochastic Systems

Directly maps conceptual frameworks to market data.

### 1. Time Series Analysis
- Autocorrelation
- Stationarity
- ARIMA models
- Volatility clustering (GARCH)
- Understanding violations of classical assumptions

### 2. Stochastic Processes
- Brownian motion
- Markov chains
- Diffusion processes
- Ito Calculus

**Core Process:**
$$dX_t = \mu dt + \sigma dW_t$$

### 3. Hidden State Modeling
Formal methods for modeling latent behavioral regimes.
- Hidden Markov Models (HMM)
- State-space models
- Kalman filters

---

## Phase 4: Financial Theory

### 1. Market Structure
- Order books and liquidity
- Execution logic
- Spreads and slippage

### 2. Derivatives
- Options, futures, swaps
- Volatility surfaces
- The Greeks and pricing models

**Core Pricing (Black-Scholes):**
$$C = S_0 N(d_1) - Ke^{-rT} N(d_2)$$

### 3. Portfolio Theory
- Diversification and covariance
- Factor exposure
- Risk-adjusted returns

## Phase 5: Machine Learning and Statistical Learning

Approach this only after mastering the foundations.
**Focus areas:**
- Regression and clustering
- Tree-based models

## Phase 6: Research Discipline

The transition from a thinker to a quantitative researcher.

### 1. Critical Questions
- Is this statistically significant?
- Is the model robust or overfit?
- Does the logic survive regime changes?
- Does it survive transaction costs?

### 2. Experimental Design
Every idea must follow a structured path:
- Hypothesis
- Measurable definition
- Testing
- Evaluation

## Suggested Learning Order

### First 6 Months: Foundations
- **Focus:** Calculus, Probability, Python, Statistics, Linear Algebra.
- **Output:** Simulations and simple models.

### Months 6–12: Intermediate
- **Focus:** Time Series, Stochastic Processes, Finance basics, Regime modeling.
- **Output:** Volatility systems, HMMs, Market state classifiers.

### Year 1–2: Advanced
- **Focus:** Stochastic Calculus, Derivatives, Optimization, ML, Execution systems.
- **Output:** Research pipelines, Portfolio systems, Probabilistic strategy frameworks.

## Mindset

**The Rule:** Every conceptual idea must eventually become measurable. 

Abstract systems thinking must be balanced with rigorous estimation to transform conceptual frameworks into viable quantitative research.
